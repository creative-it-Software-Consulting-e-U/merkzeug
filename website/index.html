Sheet 01Your filesStart where you are

One folder. Your whole collection.

A vault is simply a folder of Markdown files. Your notebook, your cloud folder, or the documentation beside your code.

  • Local & in the cloudOpen existing files without importing them into a new database. Your chosen cloud service handles synchronization.
  • Git, your wayUse repository folders on Mac and Linux or directly in IntelliJ. On iPhone and iPad, open your Git app’s repository folder through the Files app.
  • Open filesMarkdown stays Markdown. Work on the same files with other editors, Git tools, and agents.
Git on iPhone and iPad: your choice, with direct integration

Working Copy is a separate Git app for iPhone and iPad. It clones repositories and handles version control and synchronization; Merkzeug reads and edits the Markdown files inside them.

You can also use any other Git app that makes its repository folder available for opening and editing through the Files app. Perform Git operations in that app. Working Copy additionally has a direct integration: once configured, you can start Pull, Commit, and Push from Merkzeug.

Sheet 03Your layoutFrom draft to delivery

Your Markdown. Your finished document.

Turn a collection of notes into a document for clients, teams, or your next meeting. With your letterhead, your typography, and your layout.

  • One layout, many usesUse HTML/CSS templates while editing and when printing. Mac, Linux, iPhone, iPad, and IntelliJ also export PDF files with those templates.
  • Several notes, one PDFOn Mac, Linux, iPhone, iPad, and in IntelliJ, an overview document can include directly linked Markdown files from its own folder and subfolders in the export.
  • The details make the documentFine-tune exports with frontmatter: title, language, table of contents, and excluded notes. Your Markdown remains the source.
  • Share your templatesSync templates between Apple devices through iCloud, or keep them inside your vault. Choose the location directly in the PDF controls; the shared assignment travels with your Git repository.

Sheet 04AssetsKeep things together

Notes evolve. Their connections stay.

Inserted images live in a companion .assets folder beside the note. Rename or move the note — its images and every link follow.

  • Images beside the noteInserted images live in a companion .assets folder. Move or rename a note with Merkzeug and its assets follow; references are updated.
  • One heading, one filenameOn Mac and Linux, filenames can follow the first main heading. Meeting notes on Mac, Linux, iPhone, and iPad also follow their heading when it changes. IntelliJ retains control of filenames.
  • Refactoring includedMerkzeug updates local Markdown links when notes are renamed or moved. The IntelliJ plugin also adds the asset rules to the IDE’s Move/Rename actions.

Sheet 05AgentsYou keep the overview

Agents contribute. You keep the overview.

Make a readable collection out of agent-written Markdown files. Merkzeug helps you review, edit, and share the result.

  • A prompt for your layoutGive your agent a template prompt with the file structure, available fields, and styling rules, so it can adapt a template to your visual identity.
  • Pass on the rulesReview the asset guidance and existing AGENTS.md / CLAUDE.md files. Choose which files receive the instructions. Merkzeug itself does not run agents.

From calendar event to meeting note.

On Mac, iPhone, or iPad, choose an event and start with a title, time, and attendees instead of an empty page. On Linux and in IntelliJ, use ICS files or HTTPS/Webcal subscriptions; IntelliJ on Windows also reads classic Outlook. Your calendar stays unchanged. Personal notes and ideas need neither Git nor an agent.

Your notes on Linux.

Choose your processor architecture first, then the package for your distribution. Each download contains one architecture only. Updates are installed manually. If unsure, run uname -m: x86_64 means Intel/AMD, aarch64 means ARM64.

For ARM Linux, including Linux in Parallels on Apple Silicon Macs.

AppImage requires FUSE 2 and permission to use unprivileged user namespaces. On systems that restrict this, use DEB or RPM. DEB and RPM install through your package manager.
